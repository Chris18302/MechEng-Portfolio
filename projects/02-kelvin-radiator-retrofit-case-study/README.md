# Residential Radiator Retrofit — Field Engineering Case Study

**Portfolio status:** Core project — strong fit for HVAC/MEP, commissioning, field engineering, building-performance, and controls-oriented roles.

## Executive Summary

Performed field verification and installation work for residential steam-heating retrofits in occupied NYC buildings. Measured existing radiators, selected smart enclosure configurations, installed temperature sensors, adapted hardware to non-standard field conditions, and coordinated with occupants and building staff to complete installations safely and correctly. In one non-standard condition, I modified available mounting hardware to achieve a secure same-visit installation instead of deferring the work for replacement parts.

## Situation / Engineering Problem

Existing residential buildings rarely match drawings perfectly. Radiator dimensions, wall clearances, piping arrangements, and occupied-space constraints affected whether a standard enclosure configuration could be installed as planned.

## My Role

- Reviewed mechanical/plumbing plans before field work to locate radiators and understand installation conditions.
- Measured radiators and surrounding clearances in the field.
- Selected enclosure sizes/configurations based on actual dimensions.
- Installed smart radiator enclosures and mechanical components.
- Installed temperature sensors for radiator inlet, outlet, and ambient conditions.
- Worked around one-pipe and two-pipe steam heating systems.
- Coordinated with tenants, superintendents, property managers, and installation crews.
- Supported inventory, material preparation, QA/QC, and equipment reuse.

## Key Engineering Decisions and Trade-Offs

### Fit vs. Standardization
Standard components improved installation speed, but existing conditions did not always match standard dimensions. I had to determine when a standard configuration would work and when an on-site adjustment was required.

### Same-Visit Completion vs. Replacement Parts
For one radiator that did not fit the available enclosure sizes, the options were to wait for another part, defer the installation, or adapt available hardware. I identified an enclosure close to the required dimensions, used the available L-shaped mounting material to create the needed height adjustment, verified the fit, and secured the enclosure.

### Field Conditions vs. Drawings
Plans were useful for preparation, but final decisions depended on field measurements and actual site conditions.

### Occupied-Space Work
Technical installation decisions also had to account for tenant communication, safety, access, and minimizing disruption.

## Result

- Completed retrofit installations under real existing-building constraints.
- Achieved a secure same-visit installation for a non-standard radiator condition using available hardware.
- Installed sensing points that supported temperature monitoring around the radiator and occupied space.
- Strengthened practical understanding of steam heating, constructability, field verification, and retrofit QA/QC.
