# MEP & Infrastructure Coordination — Brooklyn Navy Yard Case Study

**Portfolio status:** Core project — strong fit for HVAC/MEP, commissioning, field engineering, project engineering, infrastructure, and construction-coordination roles.

## Executive Summary

Supported active mechanical and infrastructure projects across the Brooklyn Navy Yard by drafting underground utility layouts in AutoCAD, reviewing mechanical/HVAC drawings, comparing field conditions and Resident Engineer information against drawings/as-builts, and supporting construction-administration documents. The work required translating between design intent, existing conditions, and project documentation rather than treating drawings as automatically correct.

## Situation / Engineering Problem

Infrastructure projects in an existing industrial campus depend on accurate information about utilities, piping, mechanical equipment, and site conditions. Drawings, as-builts, field observations, and contractor/Resident Engineer information can differ, creating coordination risk if discrepancies are not identified.

## My Role

- Observed underground piping, steam distribution, and mechanical systems in the field.
- Reviewed mechanical/HVAC drawings for steam-line relocation, domestic-water piping, PRV/backflow vaults, restoration, and mitigation work.
- Compared field information and Resident Engineer reports with drawings and as-built documentation.
- Supported RFIs, RFPs, IFBs, submittals, bid forms, invoices/change orders, and project documentation.
- Coordinated information across operations, finance, leasing, property management, and Design & Construction teams.
- Developed and presented a self-initiated overview of NYCECC and Special Inspections.

## Key Engineering Decisions and Trade-Offs

### Drawing Intent vs. Existing Conditions
A drawing can be internally consistent and still not represent the field accurately. I learned to compare drawings against site observations and as-built information before relying on them.

### Detail vs. Usability
Utility mapping needed enough detail to support coordination without becoming unreadable or implying certainty where field information was incomplete.

### Scope / Procurement vs. Constructability
Procurement and construction documents had to be reviewed with awareness of how the work would actually be installed and coordinated with existing systems.

### Code / Inspection Awareness
My NYCECC and Special Inspections work reinforced that engineering documents also have to support compliance, inspection, and verification requirements.
