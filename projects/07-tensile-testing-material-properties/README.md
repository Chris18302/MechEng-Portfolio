# Tensile Testing & Material Properties

## Executive Summary

Analyzed tensile-test data to quantify stiffness, yielding, ultimate strength, ductility, and fracture behavior. The analysis produced an estimated **Young's modulus of 3.94 × 10⁷ psi**, **0.2% offset yield strength of 68 ksi**, **ultimate tensile strength of 82.6 ksi**, **54.9% reduction of area**, and **true fracture stress of 183 ksi**.

## Engineering Objectives

- Interpret engineering stress-strain behavior.
- Estimate Young's modulus from the elastic region.
- Determine 0.2% offset yield strength.
- Determine ultimate tensile strength.
- Calculate elongation and reduction of area.
- Compare engineering and true stress near fracture.

| Ultimate Tensile Strength | 82.6 ksi |

> Values are presented as approximate experimental results from the original academic analysis.

## Core Calculations

**Engineering stress**

```text
σ = P / A₀
```

**Engineering strain**

```text
ε = ΔL / L₀
```

**Young's modulus**

```text
E = Δσ / Δε
```

## Key Engineering Interpretation

The specimen progressed through elastic deformation, yielding, plastic deformation, strain hardening, ultimate tensile strength, necking, and fracture. The difference between engineering and true fracture stress illustrates why using the original area becomes less representative after significant necking.
