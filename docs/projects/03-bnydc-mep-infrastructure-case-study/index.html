P-02MEP / Infrastructure

Executive summary

Drafted underground utility layouts in AutoCAD, reviewed mechanical/HVAC drawings for steam and water systems, compared field conditions and Resident Engineer information against drawings and as-builts, and supported RFIs, submittals, procurement documents, and change-order workflows. The strongest lesson was that design documents must be checked against actual conditions before they are treated as ground truth.

Situation / engineering problem

Infrastructure projects on an existing industrial campus depend on accurate information about utilities, piping, mechanical equipment, and site conditions. Drawings, as-builts, field observations, and contractor or Resident Engineer information can disagree, creating coordination risk.

Actions I owned or directly performed

  • Drafted underground utility layouts in AutoCAD.
  • Observed underground piping, steam-distribution systems, and mechanical equipment in the field.
  • Reviewed mechanical/HVAC drawings for steam-line relocation, domestic-water piping, PRV/backflow vaults, and restoration/mitigation work.
  • Compared field information and Resident Engineer reports with drawings and as-built documentation.
  • Contributed to RFIs, RFPs, IFBs, submittals, bid forms, invoices/change orders, and project documentation.
  • Developed and presented a self-initiated overview of NYCECC and Special Inspections.

Key decisions & trade-offs

Drawing intent vs. existing conditions

A drawing can be internally consistent and still not represent field conditions accurately. I learned to compare drawings against site observations and as-built information before relying on them.

Detail vs. usability

Utility mapping needed enough detail to support coordination without implying certainty where field information was incomplete.

Documentation vs. constructability

Procurement and construction documents had to be understood in the context of how work would actually be installed around existing systems.

Results

  • Produced and reviewed field-informed documentation used in active infrastructure and mechanical coordination.
  • Improved my ability to recognize mismatches between documented and observed conditions.
  • Built practical experience with MEP drawings, utilities, steam/water systems, construction administration, and multidisciplinary project delivery.

Confidentiality

Employer drawings, internal project documents, site-sensitive information, and proprietary records are excluded.
