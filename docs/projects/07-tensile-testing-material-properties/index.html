P-07 Materials / Experimental Analysis

Engineering objectives

  • Construct and interpret an engineering stress-strain curve.
  • Determine Young's modulus from the elastic region.
  • Estimate 0.2% offset yield strength.
  • Determine ultimate tensile strength.
  • Calculate percent elongation and reduction of area.
  • Compare engineering stress with true stress near fracture.

Key results

Young's modulus≈ 3.94 × 10⁷ psi
0.2% offset yield≈ 68 ksi
Ultimate tensile strength≈ 82.6 ksi
Elongation · 2 in gauge≈ 23.8%
Reduction of area≈ 54.9%
True fracture stress≈ 183 ksi

Values are based on the original academic tensile-test analysis and are presented as approximate experimental results.

Core calculations

Engineering stressσ = P / A₀
Engineering strainε = ΔL / L₀
Young's modulusE = Δσ / Δε

Material behavior

The tensile response progresses through elastic deformation, yielding, plastic deformation, strain hardening, ultimate tensile strength, necking, and fracture. The results show why material selection depends on more than maximum strength: stiffness, yield behavior, ductility, and fracture characteristics also matter.

Connection to the FEA project

This project examines experimentally measured material behavior, while the stress concentration study examines how geometry affects stress and displacement numerically. Together, they demonstrate experimental and computational approaches to mechanical engineering analysis.
