Executive summary

Reviewed and organized technical building-performance content covering weatherization, heat pumps, building science, and NYC energy requirements; maintained program data in Excel; and supported training delivery. Situation / engineering problem

Improving building performance requires more than selecting efficient equipment. Engineers and technicians need to understand the envelope, HVAC systems, operating conditions, utility use, retrofit opportunities, and the policy requirements that drive upgrades.

Actions I directly performed

  • Reviewed technical course materials on building science and energy efficiency.
  • Worked with Weatherization Assistance Program content.

  • Maintained Excel trackers for progress, participation, and task completion.
  • Worked with training content covering NYC Local Laws 33, 84, 87, 88, and 97.

Key technical connections

Envelope + HVAC

Weatherization and envelope improvements can reduce heating and cooling loads, changing equipment sizing and operating requirements.

Heat pumps + electrification

Heat-pump selection depends on application, source/sink conditions, distribution systems, climate, and existing-building constraints—not just a published efficiency rating.

Benchmarking + action

Benchmarking creates visibility; engineering value comes from using the data to identify and prioritize improvements.

Policy + engineering

NYC energy requirements influence what owners measure, audit, upgrade, and report, connecting engineering analysis to capital planning and decarbonization.
